Why We Banned Symmetric Ciphers: The Case for Strict Asymmetric Storage in Threat Feeds

Traditional threat intel networks rely heavily on symmetric shared secrets (AES-GCM, ChaCha20) for speed, opening catastrophic insider risk when server nodes are subpoenaed or infiltrated. By converting our persistent datastores strictly to dual-layer RSA-3072 vaults and ElGamal Schnorr envelopes, server administrators are mathematically blinded from viewing payloads or tampering with indicators of compromise without instant cryptographic alarm.
